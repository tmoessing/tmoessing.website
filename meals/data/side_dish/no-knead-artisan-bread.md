## Ingredients
- 390 g (3 cups) bread flour (not all-purpose), plus more for dusting
- 1/4 tsp instant yeast
- 1 1/4 tsp fine sea salt
- 320 g (about 1 1/3 cups) water

## Instructions
1. In a large bowl, stir together the flour, yeast, salt, and water until a rough dough forms. If it seems very dry, add a little more water.
2. Move the dough to an oiled container and cover loosely with plastic wrap. Let it rise at room temperature (about 70°F) for 14 hours or overnight, until puffed up to about 1 1/2 to 2 times its size.
3. Lightly flour a sheet of parchment paper and set the dough on it. Fold the dough in half, then in half again.
4. Shape it into a ball by tucking the sides underneath, and set it seam side down on the parchment. Cover and let it rest until puffy but not fully risen, 30 minutes to 2 hours depending on how warm your kitchen is.
5. Preheat the oven to 500°F with a 6-quart Dutch oven inside for 20 minutes. Take the pot out, slide your hand under the parchment, and flip the dough into the pot so the seam faces up. Cover with the lid.
6. Put the pot in the oven and lower the temperature to 400°F. Bake covered for 40 minutes, then uncover and bake 15–20 minutes more, until golden brown.
7. Check that it's cooked through: tip the loaf on its side and push a thermometer into the bottom. It should read 200–205°F. If not, put it back in (extra time is fine; put the lid back on if the top is browning too fast).
8. Cool on a wire rack for at least 1 hour. It should feel light and sound hollow when you knock on the bottom. Cutting it too soon makes the crumb gummy.

## Notes
Plan ahead: the dough needs about 14 hours to rise, plus up to 2 more hours after shaping. Mix it after dinner and bake the next day.

## Tips
- If the dough isn't puffy and bubbly after the first rise, put it in the microwave with the light on and the door cracked. The warmth from the bulb gives the yeast a boost in a chilly kitchen.
- Great for sandwiches, crostini, and soaking up sauces.
